About

From planning practice to research

I have worked in supply chain operations at Zones LLC (USA) since 2022, and in inventory planning since 2024. Today I lead replenishment and distribution planning and, as Kinaxis Functional Lead, specify planning logic with the vendor's developers and test it before it goes live.

Every change to a planning rule raises the same question: did it work? A before-and-after comparison cannot separate a new rule from shifting demand. That question drives my research, which brings randomized experiments and causal inference to operations, where decisions act with delays and products share stock, capacity and suppliers.

Research interests

  • Design and analysis of experiments with delayed effects and interference, including rerandomized and model-assisted switchbacks
  • Causal evaluation of planning policies and AI decision support
  • Inventory and supply-network analytics

Research

Papers and work in progress

Methods for testing operational decisions reliably, where effects arrive with a delay and units share resources.

Working paper · 2026

Switchback experiments with delayed effects: Lead-time blindness, exposure alignment and rerandomization on the right days

When a decision acts only after a replenishment lead time, a switchback that switches at least as often as the lead time is blind: the standard contrast is centred exactly on zero, whatever the true effect. Comparing each day with the decision that set its stock recovers the effect (exactly under backlogging), and an aligned randomization test detects a safety-stock change in two-thirds of simulated one-year experiments where the conventional test has no power.

With a lead time, rerandomization should balance covariates on the lagged exposure rather than on the day: this cuts the relevant imbalance twentyfold.

Under review

Multi-objective knowledge graph completion for sustainable supplier substitution under disruption: Benchmarking learning against post-hoc filtering

R. Z. Saleem et al. · Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence (under review)

A sustainability-risk penalty inside the loss of graph neural networks, compared with post-hoc filtering and evaluated by disruption simulation with seven-seed paired tests: 33.3% lower recommended-set risk on a food network without loss of recall (p = 0.0156), while a zero-parameter certificate rule beats every learned model on another network.
